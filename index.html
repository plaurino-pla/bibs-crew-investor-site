03 · The thesis

Four levers, all validated on site

None of these requires construction, relicensing of the building, or a concept change. Combined capex: €14k — all funded at closing.

Cowork membership

The room holds 20 working positions through weekday hours that currently earn almost nothing. €129/month including daily coffee credit, power at every table, quiet hours till 12:30. No specialty-coffee cowork hybrid exists on the Linha.

Year-3 impact · ~€25k+ at ~90% margin · capex €6.5k

Lunch-grade menu

Today's menu stops at brunch — no salad bowls, no quiche, no daily warm dish. A tight lunch line raises the ticket, fills 12:30–15:00, and feeds the members already in the room. Underpins the 67% → 71% gross-margin plan.

Impact · ticket + daypart + 4pts GM · capex €4k

Books corner

Curated retail leveraging the lead investor's two decades in publishing — direct publisher relationships unlock consignment terms most cafés can't get. Modest revenue, outsized brand, dwell time and press value.

Year-3 impact · ~€13k at 38% margin · capex €3.5k

Roaster partnership

A committed specialty roaster is ready to partner: better cost per espresso on every cup, co-branded retail bags, cuppings and events that build the membership community.

Impact · margin lift + €5–9k retail · capex €0

How investors get paid

Returns here are cash distributions, not a hoped-for exit multiple. The mechanics, in order:

1 · Same terms as the lead

Invest from €5k — your stake is pro-rata

NewCo raises €80k. The lead invests €40k for 50%; co-investors share the other 50% at exactly the same price per point — invest what you want from €5k, and your stake is simply your investment ÷ €80k. No management fees, no carry, no salary from NewCo.

2 · Quarterly dividends

All cash above a €25k buffer

Each quarter, every euro of cash above a fixed €25k operating buffer is distributed pro-rata to quotas. Nothing accumulates in the company beyond the buffer. In the base case, first dividends flow in Year 2 — model your own ticket below.

3 · Capital back, then yield

Dividends count against your capital first

We report every distribution against your invested capital until it is fully returned; everything after that is yield. Dividends alone repay slowly on the base case — which is why the structure includes a defined buyout path, not just a hoped-for sale.

4 · Two exit doors

Repurchase if we expand · trespasse if we sell

If NewCo expands — a second site, brand licensing — the company or the lead repurchases co-investor quotas at a pre-agreed valuation formula, so you can cash out without waiting for a sale. Otherwise, a future trespasse sale of the establishment distributes pro-rata after debts. Governance: quarterly P&L against this model, annual accounts, investor consent on reserved matters.

07 · Risks, stated plainly

What can go wrong

If any of these makes you walk away, that's the correct use of this page.

  • It has never been profitable. The thesis depends on executing new revenue streams, not inheriting earnings. The downside preset above shows a path that never breaks even in three years — the €12k contingency and fully-funded capex exist for exactly that path, with the lead able to bridge beyond it before any new money dilutes anyone.
  • Founder dependency. Part of today's goodwill is personal to the founder. Mitigants: paid 4-week handover, 3-year local non-compete, retention of permanent staff — and a named day-to-day operator signed before closing, as a hard condition.
  • Seasonality. Winter trades ~20% below average. The cowork stream is deliberately counter-seasonal — members are weekday locals, not tourists.
  • Rent step-up. +€3,000/yr from July 2026. Already in every scenario on this page.
  • Licensing transfer. The esplanada (24 of 60 seats) must be re-issued to NewCo by CM Oeiras — a closing condition, not a post-closing hope.
  • Exit reality. Small F&B businesses trade at low multiples. Returns here come from cash distributions and optionality (second site, brand licensing), not multiple expansion. Model it that way.
